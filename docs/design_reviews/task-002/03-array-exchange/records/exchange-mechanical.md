# Automatic exchange: independent mechanical assessment

**PROPOSED; 2 October 2026. No new measured results.** Read constitution, project state, Task 002 requirements brief, mechanical role and prior mechanical architecture comparison. The new human instruction makes finished-array unloading and fresh-array loading part of automatic operation; the old requirements brief's narrower reading is superseded for this assessment.

**ENGINEERING JUDGMENT — direct recommendation:** All three layouts can automate exchange. Begin with a common, upright removable carrier that has dedicated handling features, a mechanically latched nest, separate input/output pockets, and a protected envelope around the installed sticks. Do not choose moving-holder motion merely because exchange is required: every layout must transport completed arrays at least once. Moving-holder motion adds repeated transport during population; its acceptability depends on installed-stick retention and collision testing. Fixed holders avoid that repeated motion, but they still require the same retention test for unloading.

| Layout | Automatic exchange integration | Distinctive mechanical gate |
|---|---|---|
| Carried fine stage / fixed holder | Retract precision tool, use a separate carrier grip or automatically change tool, remove carrier and replace it in stationary nest | Carrier handling hardware must not compromise delicate stick grip or wrist-supported alignment |
| Supported head / moving XY holder | Park XY at a repeatable accessible exchange position; clear head; exchange carrier on stage | Stage must carry clamp/carrier mass and the full populated envelope. Acceleration, settling, retention and neighbor sweeps occur on every indexing move |
| Supported tool / fixed holder | Park supported tool outside handling corridor; UR exchanges carrier at stationary nest | Full array tool travel and parking clearance required; if UR also delivers stick tool, unattended recoupling must be demonstrated |

## Constrain the carrier; do not clamp the sticks

**SOURCE-BASED FACT:** Carr Lane describes six-contact 3-2-1 location, separating locators from clamping and directing forces into supported locations. Its application is machining fixtures, not proof of micron insertion performance. [Manufacturer fixture principles](https://www.carrlane.com/engineering-resources/fixture-design-principles/locating-clamping-principles/ctl).

**ENGINEERING JUDGMENT:** For a sufficiently rigid rectangular carrier: three separated bottom pads establish Z, pitch and roll; two side contacts establish lateral position and yaw; one end contact establishes the remaining translation. Compliant side pushers seat the carrier against the side/end locators; an automatic hold-down seats it against bottom pads. Apply hold-down over supported rigid carrier regions to avoid bowing the holder/channel plane. Include lead-in chamfers and clearance for robot placement, but locate finally on defined pads rather than wedging into four close-fitting walls. Pins may substitute if actual holder geometry supplies appropriate datums; round-plus-relieved/diamond location avoids two tight round pins fighting spacing variation. Do not specify a numeric repeatability or preload without hardware measurements.

**OPEN QUESTION:** Holder geometry, material, allowable contact pressure, underside access, stick protrusion below the holder, mass, channel depth, retention and permitted modifications are unknown. Carrier-to-holder attachment is a second joint: locate and fasten the holder without distortion; either measure actual holder/channel fiducials after every loading or establish their stability through tests. A carrier fiducial alone cannot reveal holder slip or clamp-induced deformation.

**CALCULATION — sizing framework, not results:** Resolve handling loads as F=ma and weight mg for the actual loaded mass. For friction-only carrier jaws with summed normal force N, require mu*N >= S*F_parallel using measured worst-case friction and justified safety factor S; check tipping moment and jaw/contact stress separately. Prefer a positive undercut/hook or supported carrier ledge to reduce dependence on friction. For a moving stage, clamp preload must maintain every locating contact under acceleration and off-center mass moments. Insertion reaction must pass through local holder/carrier support to the base. No insertion-force, mass, friction, stiffness or allowable clamp-force values are supplied, so numerical preload and actuator selection are premature.

## Populated-array handling

**SOURCE-BASED FACT:** SCHUNK describes robot loading of fixtures/pallets as a distinct alternative to directly handling individual workpieces. This establishes industrial prior art, not affordability or suitability of its commercial system. [Manufacturer machine-tending description](https://schunk.com/sg/en/applications/machine-tending).

**ENGINEERING JUDGMENT:** Dedicated side ears or an outside carrier rim let the robot grip below/outside the occupied-stick envelope. Grip the carrier, never the installed sticks. Prefer upright transfer, low acceleration and a mechanically supported output pocket; do not assume a vacuum cup has an accessible sealed patch on an unknown populated holder. A removable protective frame can prevent accidental side contact, but a lid pressing on sticks can itself damage or displace them. A channel-clearance fit does not establish retention. Even stationary population requires evidence that gravity, release forces and one subsequent carrier transfer will not eject, tilt or fracture sticks.

**OPEN QUESTION:** If sticks protrude below open channels, carrier support must avoid their ends. If retention is absent, determine whether a noncontact underside catcher, permitted backing plate, process retention step or upright constrained transport is allowed. Do not silently add adhesive or spring retention to the product. Holder acceleration can load an unsupported protruding stick even without visible axial loss; inspect cracks, displacement and tilt after transfer.

**ENGINEERING JUDGMENT — failure cases:** Debris on pads changes Z/tilt; overclamping bends channels; an inverted/wrong carrier seats falsely; a high stick snags head/gripper; a partly loaded array must leave intact after an aborted insertion; gripper and nest can both constrain the carrier during release; a power loss can drop an unlocked carrier; output pocket fullness can strand the completed holder. Use keyed carrier orientation, accessible clean pads, defined head/tool parking, positive grip confirmation, automatic latch-state verification and supported quarantine/output pockets. Sensors establish state, not proof of micron seating; verify pose after every clamp cycle. Avoid unlimited retries after a jam.

## Smallest useful verification

**ENGINEERING JUDGMENT:** First build a manual carrier/nest surrogate to test bottom clearance, supported clamp paths, populated-neighbor clearance and actual retention. Next demonstrate UR upright pickup, removal, output placement and replacement with representative populated surrogate arrays. Measure holder pose before/after clamping and after repeated exchange; inspect actual parts once surrogate transport is satisfactory. Separately compare moving-stage indexing with stationary-array tool motion at the intended acceleration profile. Test partial fills, tallest stick, corner/interior channels, contaminated locator, missing carrier, failed latch and occupied output pocket. This distinguishes repeated-motion risk from one-time unloading risk without building all three complete cells.

**ENGINEERING JUDGMENT — cost/schedule/confidence:** Carrier ears, passive nests and fabricated pockets are plausible low-complexity starts with available machining, but automatic latch, carrier grip/tool change, sensing and recovery add scope to all layouts. No priced BOM establishes compliance with the $1,500 total budget. Retention/holder drawings and carrier experiment should precede precision-stage purchase. High confidence that carrier-level handling decouples fragile sticks from exchange gripping; low confidence in allowable acceleration/preload or any architecture's automatic exchange reliability until tests. Chief Engineer retains selection authority.
